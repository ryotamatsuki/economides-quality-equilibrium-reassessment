## 2. Current journal evidence

### Review of Industrial Organization — strongest fit

Official scope: https://link.springer.com/journal/11151/aims-and-scope

Current scope states that RIO publishes research on all aspects of industrial organization, with a main focus on competition and monopoly and their effects on efficiency and innovation. It retains a primary interest in ideas connected to empirical/case/real conditions but explicitly also seeks papers advancing significant IO theories and policy, and welcomes shorter notes and commentaries.

Recent fit evidence:

- Cohen, A. and Heifetz, A. (2024), “Location, Location, Quality: The Fixed Differentiation Principle,” *Review of Industrial Organization* 65, 705–720. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11151-024-09989-3
- Sorek, G. (2026), “Quality Innovation with Variable Demand Elasticity,” *Review of Industrial Organization* 68, 311–325. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11151-025-10046-w

Cohen–Heifetz is especially material. It explicitly places Economides (1989) in the vertical/horizontal differentiation literature, but its model is a **location-then-price game with exogenous quality asymmetry and quadratic transportation costs**, whereas the present paper studies the **original three-stage location–endogenous-quality–price game with linear transportation costs** and the global continuation after endogenous quality deviations. It therefore does not absorb the frozen contribution, but it is close enough that omission would create an avoidable referee-positioning risk.

RIO fit assessment: **VERY HIGH audience fit / MEDIUM editorial risk**. The main risk is contribution narrowness and prior-art positioning, not topic mismatch.

### Regional Science and Urban Economics — plausible but current-fit risk

Official scope: https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/regional-science-and-urban-economics/0166-0462

RSUE seeks significant theoretical or empirical contributions with a spatial dimension. It remains receptive to theoretical spatial work. Recent relevant evidence includes:

- Colombo, S. and Lambertini, L. (2023), “R&D investments with spillovers and endogenous horizontal differentiation,” *Regional Science and Urban Economics* 98, 103861. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0166046222000990

This demonstrates that multistage Hotelling/R&D theory remains within scope. The original Economides (1989) article was itself published in RSUE, giving the reassessment a strong historical connection. However, the current paper's surviving result is mainly an IO equilibrium correction at fixed maximal locations rather than a new regional/urban mechanism. Current RSUE positioning therefore requires an editor to regard product-characteristic space and correction of an RSUE antecedent as sufficient spatial significance.

RSUE fit assessment: **MEDIUM-HIGH audience fit / MEDIUM-HIGH desk risk**. Treat as a stretch/historical-fit option, not the default first submission.

### International Journal of Industrial Organization — topical fit, contribution-threshold risk

Official scope: https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/international-journal-of-industrial-organization/0167-7187

IJIO explicitly covers theoretical and empirical industrial organization, including classic strategic behavior and market structure. Recent work confirms that endogenous quality investment and equilibrium-existence theory remain within its remit; for example:

- “Digital business models and quality investment,” *International Journal of Industrial Organization* 103 (2025), 103204. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167718725000700

Thus scope is not the problem. The difficulty is contribution threshold: recent IJIO theory typically supplies a broader new mechanism, application, or general result. Stage 11 intentionally narrowed the present manuscript after Bunte/Sorenson prior-art review. Attempting to manufacture broader claims for IJIO would violate the theory freeze.

IJIO fit assessment: **HIGH topic fit / HIGH desk and referee contribution-level risk**. Keep only as an optional high-risk stretch.

### Journal of Economics — realistic theoretical fallback

Official scope: https://link.springer.com/journal/712/aims-and-scope

The journal publishes across economics and states that both micro- and macroeconomic papers often have a theoretical/mathematical focus. A recent spatial-theory example is:

- Bai, N. and Matsumura, T. (2023), “Common ownership in a delivered pricing duopoly,” *Journal of Economics* 139, 191–208. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00712-023-00822-1

This indicates a credible audience for technically self-contained Hotelling/location-price theory and welfare analysis.

Journal of Economics fit assessment: **HIGH methodological fit / MODERATE contribution risk**, and therefore a defensible realistic fallback if RIO declines the paper.

## 4. Primary-target rationale — RIO

RIO dominates the alternatives on the combination that matters for this manuscript:

1. the paper is unambiguously industrial organization;
