## What Bunte already establishes

Bunte is not merely adjacent literature. Chapter 3 explicitly compares his analysis with Economides (1989).

- On p. 92, Bunte says that Economides obtains maximum differentiation despite using essentially the same model and attributes the difference to Economides not imposing a stability condition on the R&D decision. Bunte states that imposing the stability condition leaves no parameter values for maximum differentiation in his Hotelling scenario.
- The same discussion distinguishes three resolutions of linear-Hotelling price instability: the d’Aspremont-Gabszewicz-Thisse quadratic-transport solution, a Hotelling scenario based on recognition of mutual dependence, and a limit-price scenario.
- On p. 106, Bunte states that he employs the same specifications as Economides and that the derivations correspond, but again excludes Economides’s maximum-differentiation parameter region for stability reasons.
- The p. 106 footnote says Sorenson (1995) uses the same specification as Economides for the R&D problem, reports the same stability condition as Bunte, and does not address the variety decision.

Therefore the present project must not claim:

- first discovery that Economides (1989) has a stability problem;
- first challenge to the maximum-differentiation result;
- first recognition that the quality/R&D choice needs a global stability check.

## What differs from the present paper

The current paper freezes a narrower equilibrium object.

| Dimension | Economides (1989) reassessment here | Bunte (1995/1997) | Sorenson (1995) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Horizontal location/variety | Endogenous in original three-stage game; headline correction conditions on maximal locations | Endogenous variety analyzed under alternative price-instability scenarios | Not the focus; Bunte explicitly says Sorenson does not address variety choice |
| Quality/R&D | Endogenous, chosen before price | Endogenous | Endogenous product-improving R&D |
| Price behavior | Original noncooperative price game retained and re-solved after every quality deviation | Price instability handled through Hotelling mutual-dependence and limit-price scenarios | Differentiated Bertrand competition after R&D |
| Global quality deviation across price regimes | Explicitly evaluated | Stability critique is central, but the cited Chapter-3 results are derived under the alternative pricing scenarios | Stability of R&D investment is central |
| Piecewise maximal-location price continuation | Explicit theorem with shared-market and exclusion branches | Not identified in the checked Chapter-3 comparison as the paper’s object | Not identified in publisher abstract |
| Exact `4/27` threshold in the present normalization | Derived here from the original-game exclusion deviation | No equivalent threshold located in the checked Bunte material | No equivalent threshold located in publisher metadata/abstract |
| Explicit quality equilibria `(1/lambda,0)` and mirror | Derived here | No identical pair located in checked Bunte material | No identical pair located in publisher metadata/abstract |
| Variety conclusion | No new full location equilibrium claimed | Hotelling scenario: minimum differentiation; limit-price scenario: intermediate differentiation | Variety decision not analyzed according to Bunte |

## Non-absorption conclusion

Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE BUT NON-ABSORBING`, subject to the scope below.

Bunte absorbs the broad criticism that Economides’s quality/R&D calculation lacks a stability check. That criticism is prior art and is not a contribution of the current paper. What remains is model-specific and narrower: retain the original noncooperative price game, derive its maximal-location price equilibrium over the full quality-gap domain, and show that regime-changing quality deviations imply the exact `4/27` threshold and explicit asymmetric exclusion quality equilibria.

Sorenson reinforces the prior-art status of the R&D-stability objection. It does not absorb the endogenous-variety part of the reassessment because Bunte explicitly states that Sorenson does not address the variety decision, and the publisher abstract frames Sorenson as an R&D-before-Bertrand leadership paper.
