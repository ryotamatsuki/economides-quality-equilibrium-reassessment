\section{Exact Rescaling with a General Linear Transportation Coefficient}\label{sec:robustness}

The baseline normalizes the linear transportation coefficient to one. This normalization can be relaxed without changing the strategic structure. Replace \eqref{eq:utility} by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:utility-t}
 u_i(z)=k+a_i-p_i-t|z-\ell_i|,
 \qquad t>0.
\end{equation}
At maximal locations, the regular price continuation becomes
\[
 p_1=t+\frac{\delta}{3},
 \qquad
 p_2=t-\frac{\delta}{3},
\]
and the exclusion boundary is $|\delta|=3t$.

The reported symmetric quality on the regular branch remains $A=1/(3\lambda)$, while the exclusionary quality choice remains $1/\lambda$. Repeating the global payoff comparison yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gain-t}
 \frac{\pi_i(1/\lambda,A)-\pi_i(A,A)}{\mu}
 =\frac{4-27\lambda t}{18\lambda}.
\end{equation}
Thus the private regime-switch threshold is the dimensionless condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:threshold-t}
 \lambda t=\frac4{27}.
\end{equation}
The welfare and consumer-surplus differences similarly become
\begin{align}
 \frac{W_E-W_S}{\mu}&=\frac{10-9\lambda t}{36\lambda},\label{eq:w-t}\\
 \frac{CS_E-CS_S}{\mu}&=\frac{21\lambda t-4}{12\lambda}.\label{eq:cs-t}
\end{align}
Accordingly, the consumer-surplus threshold rescales to $\lambda t=4/21$.

This section establishes exact parametric rescaling within the class of linear transportation costs. It does not establish robustness to quadratic transportation costs, arbitrary transportation technologies, or arbitrary convex quality-cost functions. Those would change the theoretical object and are outside the frozen scope of the paper.
