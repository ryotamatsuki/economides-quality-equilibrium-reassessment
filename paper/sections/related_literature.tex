\section{Related Literature}\label{sec:literature}

The starting point is \citet{Economides1989}, who studies quality choice in a Hotelling duopoly and reports maximal variety differentiation together with minimal quality differentiation. In the three-stage location--quality--price game, the reported pure-strategy solution is obtained by solving the price stage on the regular shared-market branch, substituting those prices into quality-stage profits, and then solving the resulting first-order conditions. Our reassessment keeps that original noncooperative pricing game but evaluates quality choices against the full downstream price continuation, including exclusion branches.

The closest prior critique is \citet{Bunte1995} and the corresponding Chapter 3 discussion in \citet{Bunte1997}. This prior critique is more direct than a generic observation about Hotelling instability. Bunte states that his specifications and derivations correspond to those of \citet{Economides1989}, but argues that the maximum-differentiation parameter region is excluded by a stability condition on the R\&D game. That observation preempts any firstness claim about the stability problem in Economides. It is also important, however, to distinguish the equilibrium objects being analyzed. Bunte's Chapter 3 explicitly resolves downstream price instability through alternative pricing scenarios: a Hotelling scenario in which firms recognize mutual dependence and a separate limit-price scenario. The present paper instead asks for the pure Nash price continuation of the original Economides pricing game after every quality pair at the reported maximal locations.

The two analyses can be compared quantitatively. Let $m_B$ denote Bunte's coefficient on quadratic quality-improvement cost and let $u_B$ denote his utility loss per unit horizontal distance. With Bunte's consumer density normalized to one, the common symmetric benchmark maps to our notation through $a=\Delta Q$, $c=2m_B$, $\lambda=2m_B$, and $t=u_B$. Hence $\theta\equiv\lambda t=2m_Bu_B$. Bunte describes the Economides maximum-differentiation region as $1<18m_Bu_B<2$, which maps exactly to $1/9<\theta<2/9$, and states that his Hotelling-scenario R\&D stability condition requires $18m_Bu_B>2$, or $\theta>2/9$. That condition is therefore not algebraically identical to the finite branch-changing quality-deviation threshold $\theta=4/27$ derived in Proposition~\ref{prop:correction}. This distinction does not, by itself, establish firstness of the present threshold; it establishes only that the particular Bunte stability cutoff documented in Chapter 3 is a different condition.

\citet{Sorenson1995} is likewise relevant to the prior R\&D-stability literature. The publisher abstract establishes that firms invest in product-improving R\&D before differentiated Bertrand competition and studies persistence of market leadership, while Bunte cites Sorenson in connection with the same R\&D stability restriction. We therefore do not use the presence or absence of a variety-choice stage as a non-absorption argument. The relevant comparison for this paper is the fixed-location quality/price continuation game itself. Our results below establish that continuation and its implications within the present model; they should not be read as a claim that no algebraically equivalent result appears in inaccessible portions of the closest prior sources.

This paper also relates to the broader literature combining horizontal and vertical product differentiation. \citet{NevenThisse1990} allow firms to choose horizontal and vertical product characteristics before price competition and obtain max--min patterns of differentiation. \citet{Economides1993} studies quality variation in a circular model with entry and location decisions. \citet{DegryseIrmen2001} examine quality provision when quality changes perceived horizontal differentiation, while \citet{PigaPoyagoTheotoky2005} study a location--R\&D--price game with location-dependent R\&D spillovers. These papers establish that quality and horizontal differentiation interact strategically. The narrower object here is the original \citet{Economides1989} endpoint price continuation and the quality incentives generated by its regime changes.

Finally, the price-stage discontinuity is connected to the classic instability of linear Hotelling price competition emphasized by \citet{DAspremontGabszewiczThisse1979}. We use that fact only to clarify the modern SPNE interpretation in Section~\ref{subsec:spne}. The headline results do not depend on resolving the full location game; they condition on the maximal locations reported by the original theorem and reassess the reduced quality game at those locations. The contribution is therefore deliberately narrow: it does not replace the prior stability literature, and it does not infer novelty from the failure to locate a result in an unavailable source.
