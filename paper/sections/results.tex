\section{Price Regimes and Quality Incentives}\label{sec:results}

We now impose the maximal locations reported in the original three-stage solution,
\[
 x=0,\qquad y=1,
\]
and study the resulting price and quality subgames globally. Let
\[
 \delta\equiv a_1-a_2
\]
denote the quality gap.

\subsection{The price continuation}\label{subsec:price}

If both firms serve positive market shares, the indifferent consumer is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zhat-max}
 \hat z=\frac{1+\delta-p_1+p_2}{2}.
\end{equation}
The interior first-order conditions give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular-prices}
 p_1^{I}=1+\frac{\delta}{3},
 \qquad
 p_2^{I}=1-\frac{\delta}{3}.
\end{equation}
These are the familiar regular-branch prices. They are not, however, the correct continuation for arbitrary quality gaps.

\begin{lemma}[Maximal-location price continuation]\label{lem:price}
At $x=0$ and $y=1$, the pure-strategy price equilibrium is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:price-piecewise}
 (p_1^*,p_2^*)=
 \begin{cases}
 (0,-\delta-1), & \delta\leq -3,\\[1mm]
 \left(1+\delta/3,\,1-\delta/3\right), & -3\leq\delta\leq 3,\\[1mm]
 (\delta-1,0), & \delta\geq 3.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Thus the downstream game switches from shared-market pricing to exclusion when the absolute quality gap reaches three.
\end{lemma}

The regular candidate is globally stable against a full-market undercut precisely on its valid branch. In normalized revenue units,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square1}
 R_1^{I}-R_1^{\mathrm{capture}}
 =\frac{(\delta-3)^2}{18},
\end{equation}
and symmetrically
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square2}
 R_2^{I}-R_2^{\mathrm{capture}}
 =\frac{(\delta+3)^2}{18}.
\end{equation}
The square terms make clear that the issue is not a hidden profitable price deviation inside the regular region. The issue arises one stage earlier: a sufficiently large quality deviation moves the subsequent price game to a different branch.

Substituting Lemma~\ref{lem:price} into firm 1's payoff and dividing by $\mu$ gives the continuation value
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v1-piecewise}
 v_1(a_1,a_2)=
 \begin{cases}
 -\dfrac{\lambda a_1^2}{2}, & a_1-a_2\leq -3,\\[2mm]
 \dfrac12\left(1+\dfrac{a_1-a_2}{3}\right)^2-\dfrac{\lambda a_1^2}{2}, & |a_1-a_2|\leq 3,\\[3mm]
 a_1-a_2-1-\dfrac{\lambda a_1^2}{2}, & a_1-a_2\geq 3.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The continuation value is continuous at the regime boundary but not globally represented by the middle expression. In particular, the marginal revenue from quality jumps upward when the high-quality firm crosses from the interior branch to the exclusion branch. Local concavity of the regular branch therefore does not by itself establish a global quality best response.

\subsection{The reported symmetric quality profile}\label{subsec:symmetric}

The original quality-stage calculation substitutes the regular prices into profits and obtains, at maximal locations,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A}
 A\equiv \frac{1}{3\lambda}
\end{equation}
as the symmetric quality candidate. The following proposition gives the global incentive check within the parameter interval used in the original theorem.

\begin{proposition}[Global-deviation correction]\label{prop:correction}
Fix $x=0$, $y=1$, and $\lambda\in(1/9,2/9)$. The reported symmetric profile $(A,A)$, where $A=1/(3\lambda)$, is a Nash equilibrium of the quality subgame if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric-survival}
 \lambda\in\left[\frac{4}{27},\frac{2}{9}\right).
\end{equation}
For
\begin{equation}\label{eq:failure-region}
 \frac19<\lambda<\frac4{27},
\end{equation}
the unilateral quality choice $a_i'=1/\lambda$ is strictly profitable and induces the exclusion branch of the price subgame. The normalized gain is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global-gain}
 \frac{\pi_i(a_i',A)-\pi_i(A,A)}{\mu}
 =\frac{4-27\lambda}{18\lambda}>0.
\end{equation}
At $\lambda=4/27$, the reported action and this exclusionary action yield the same payoff.
\end{proposition}

The economics of Proposition~\ref{prop:correction} is straightforward. The regular first-order condition selects $A$ by considering marginal movements that preserve shared-market pricing. But the quality strategy set permits a finite move to $1/\lambda$. For $\lambda<2/9$ that move creates a quality gap exceeding three, so the downstream equilibrium changes from \eqref{eq:regular-prices} to the exclusion branch in \eqref{eq:price-piecewise}. The private comparison is therefore between two different price regimes, not between two points on the same smooth objective.

\subsection{Additional asymmetric quality equilibria}\label{subsec:asymmetric}

The same branch structure also generates additional pure quality equilibria.

\begin{proposition}[Asymmetric exclusion equilibria]\label{prop:asymmetric}
Fix $x=0$ and $y=1$. For every
\[
 \lambda\in\left(\frac19,\frac29\right),
\]
the quality subgame admits the two asymmetric Nash equilibria
\begin{equation}\label{eq:asymmetric-equilibria}
 \left(\frac1\lambda,0\right)
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 \left(0,\frac1\lambda\right).
\end{equation}
In either equilibrium the high-quality firm serves the market through the exclusion price continuation.
\end{proposition}

The best-response logic is particularly transparent. Against zero quality, the regular-branch payoff is increasing through the boundary $a=3$ when $\lambda<2/9$, while the exclusion-branch payoff
\[
 a-1-\frac{\lambda a^2}{2}
\]
is maximized at $a=1/\lambda$. Hence $BR(0)=1/\lambda$. Conversely, against quality $1/\lambda$, choosing zero gives the lagging firm zero profit. Remaining excluded with positive quality is costly, entering the regular branch yields a negative payoff at its lower boundary and a decreasing continuation thereafter, and leapfrogging into the opposite exclusion branch is also unprofitable. Thus $BR(1/\lambda)=0$.

Proposition~\ref{prop:asymmetric} is an existence result, not a complete characterization of the quality-equilibrium correspondence. In particular, we do not claim that \eqref{eq:asymmetric-equilibria}, together with the symmetric profile when it survives, exhaust all pure quality equilibria. What follows immediately is enough for the reassessment: the reported symmetric profile fails over part of the claimed interval, and over the remainder it coexists with at least two asymmetric exclusion equilibria.
