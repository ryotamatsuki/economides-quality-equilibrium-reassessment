\begin{abstract}
Economides (1989) reports maximal variety differentiation and minimal quality differentiation in a three-stage location--quality--price game. We retain the original noncooperative pricing structure and re-examine the quality stage using the correct downstream continuation over the full quality-gap domain. At maximal locations, the price equilibrium switches from an interior shared-market branch to exclusion when the quality gap reaches three. This regime switch invalidates the reported symmetric quality profile $a_1=a_2=1/(3\lambda)$ for $1/9<\lambda<4/27$: a finite deviation to $a_i=1/\lambda$ moves the price game to exclusion and is strictly profitable. Moreover, throughout the original interval $(1/9,2/9)$ the quality subgame admits the asymmetric exclusion equilibria $(1/\lambda,0)$ and $(0,1/\lambda)$. On the coexistence region $[4/27,2/9)$, these certified exclusion equilibria yield higher total welfare than the reported symmetric equilibrium. We also note that, under the standard modern full-history SPNE definition, feasible price subgames without pure Nash equilibria rule out a pure-strategy SPNE for the full game. The main correction, however, does not depend on that interpretation: it follows from global quality deviations within the original pricing game.
\end{abstract}
