Dear Editors of *Bulletin of Economic Research*,

I would like to submit my manuscript, “Quality Differentiation and Market Exclusion: A Note on Economides (1989),” for consideration as a short theoretical note in *Bulletin of Economic Research*.

The note revisits the quality-choice continuation of Economides’s (1989) linear Hotelling duopoly, conditioning on the two firms being located at opposite endpoints. Its contribution is an exact, necessary-and-sufficient characterization of **all pure Nash equilibria of the fixed-endpoint quality game** once the downstream price equilibrium is computed across both market-sharing and market-exclusion regimes. For the original cost interval \(1/9<\lambda<2/9\), two asymmetric exclusion equilibria exist throughout, whereas a symmetric market-sharing equilibrium joins them exactly when \(\lambda\geq4/27\), including the threshold. Thus relatively inexpensive quality investment removes the symmetric outcome but does not remove pure equilibria; more expensive investment introduces coexistence rather than a simple switch from exclusion to sharing. This distinction between the global equilibrium correspondence and local quality-adjustment instability is relevant to readers interested in product differentiation, strategic investment, and oligopoly equilibrium. Previous research, notably Bunte (1997), already questioned the stability of Economides’s quality choice; this note does not claim priority for that criticism. Its more narrowly specified contribution is the exhaustive fixed-endpoint pure-equilibrium classification, rather than an equilibrium of the full location–quality–price game.

The manuscript is intentionally concise and includes the proof of the global price continuation, the exclusion of additional pure-quality equilibria, and a reproducibility statement distinguishing mathematical arguments, exact-arithmetic checks, and earlier Lean verification of the encoded reduced payoff. The manuscript also acknowledges the earlier closely related work of Bunte (1995, 1997) and Sorenson (1995), without asserting historical priority over those studies.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
